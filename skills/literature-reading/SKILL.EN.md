# literature reading

[简体中文](SKILL.md) · English

Support this reading pattern. The user first reads an AI summary, then browses the paper's figures independently. Codex continues with figure-by-figure restatements and the full English–Chinese parallel text for the user to return to and read closely. Use Simplified Chinese for summaries and restatements, retaining essential English terminology.

## Content boundaries

- At every stage, use only the current paper's main text, figures, tables, captions, and any appendices or supplementary materials obtained for that paper. Present only information explicitly stated or labeled in those sources and explanations, views, and conclusions explicitly expressed by the authors.
- Do not add outside knowledge, content from other papers, general background, terminology tutorials, analogies, reading advice, or personal reflections. Restate background and other studies mentioned in the paper according to the authors' wording, without independently looking them up or expanding on them.
- Do not independently derive results, complete causal chains, explain mechanisms, establish relationships between figures, or suggest further applications from the paper's content. Even if a judgment seems reasonable, obvious, or calculable from the data, omit it unless the authors explicitly express it. Preserve original numerical values without independently calculating differences, ratios, effect sizes, or estimating values from figures.
- Do not evaluate novelty, strength of evidence, research quality, experimental design, limitations, applicability, or what a figure cannot establish. Restate these matters as the authors' views only when the authors explicitly discuss them, without adding independent judgments.
- Omit information the original paper does not address. Do not add entries such as “not reported in the paper,” “not discussed by the authors,” or “requires further verification,” or fill fixed sections with additional material. Always retain the five fixed summary headings and their navigation links. If the source provides no content for a heading, leave its body empty, without inventing content or adding a missing-content notice. Preserve ambiguity in the original wording without resolving it through speculation.
- Faithfully preserve attribution and the authors' tone, including hypotheses, speculation, uncertainty, and inferences made by the authors themselves. Wording such as “the authors consider/propose/report” may be used. Do not strengthen the authors' speculation into established fact.
- Summaries, figure explanations, and translations in the reading materials contain only the paper content described above and necessary section headings, page numbers, figure numbers, alignment identifiers, and reading-navigation controls. Interface labels such as “阅读导航” and “返回目录” may be used for navigation and layout, without introducing background, learning paths, or analysis absent from the paper. Briefly report necessary execution status in the chat, such as file links, unreadable materials, unclear recognition, or unfinished work. Any unavoidable markers for missing text serve only to locate the gap and must not become commentary on the paper.

## Execution pace and scope

- By default, complete the sequence continuously, from summary to figure-by-figure analysis consisting only of restatement, then to the full English–Chinese parallel text. Immediately save the completed summary as `summary.html` and provide its link in the chat, then continue with the remaining two stages. Provide `figures.html` when the figure restatements are complete and `bilingual.html` when the full parallel text is complete. A brief summary may also appear in the chat. Do not wait until the entire translation is finished to deliver the summary, or require the user to reply “continue” or confirm that they have finished viewing the figures between stages.
- Follow explicit requests for a particular stage, selected sections, a different order, or a specified format, without forcing the full workflow. When reusing existing summaries or figure explanations, verify the paper version and remove outside content or independent judgments according to the content boundaries above.
- Continue making progress within the current task. Do not claim that a background task has started or that work will continue after the user leaves unless such an execution mechanism actually exists.
- Prefer the PDF, attachments, or full-text link supplied by the user. If only an abstract is available, a summary explicitly limited to the abstract may be provided first. Clearly identify unavailable full text or images and request the corresponding materials, without fabricating subsequent figure explanations or a full translation.
- When handling multiple papers, organize each paper's sources, summary, figure explanations, and translation separately. Clarify priorities when the number of papers affects delivery order.

## Reading and locating the source

1. Confirm the title, authors, year, any existing DOI or link, and the version used from the source materials. Do not search other sources to add background. The full text of the same paper may be obtained. Read the full-text structure, key methods, results, discussion, and captions before forming a summary. Do not present findings based only on an abstract as conclusions from reading the full paper.
2. Maintain a concise coverage inventory of sections, the location of methods, figure and panel numbers, tables, appendices, and supplied supplementary materials. Use it to prevent omissions without overwhelming the user with the extraction process.
3. Combine PDF text extraction with page images. Check two-column reading order, paragraphs spanning pages, ligatures, footnotes, and equations. Scanned documents may require OCR first. Check uncertain numbers, symbols, and figure labels against the page images.
4. Provide retrievable source locations for important conclusions and every figure, such as “PDF page 7, Fig. 3b.” When printed page numbers differ from PDF page order, specify which numbering is used.
5. Include supplementary materials in restatements and translations only when they have actually been obtained and belong to the current paper. Mention unavailable materials in the chat. Preserve the original attribution of citations to other papers, without independently verifying, expanding, or rewriting them as new evidence.

## Stage 1 — Chinese summary

The top-level content sections and left-hand navigation in `summary.html` must use exactly the following five Chinese labels in the same order. Keep their names and order fixed, with no additional content sections.

1. **研究背景 (Research background)**. Restate the background, prior work, questions, and gaps described by the authors.
2. **研究内容 (Research content)**. Restate the research undertaken, subjects, tasks, and work performed by the authors, explaining what they studied.
3. **研究方法 (Research methods)**. Restate the data, samples, experiments, models, technical procedures, controls, and evaluation methods used by the authors, explaining how they conducted the study.
4. **研究目的 (Research objectives)**. Restate research objectives or problems explicitly identified by the authors, without inferring objectives from methods or results.
5. **研究结论 (Research conclusions)**. Restate the main findings and explicit conclusions reported by the authors. Retain explicitly stated significance, qualifying conditions, or limitations alongside the relevant conclusions, without creating separate sections for novelty, significance, or limitations.

Use `summary-background`, `summary-content`, `summary-methods`, `summary-objectives`, and `summary-conclusions` as the respective section anchors. Navigation links only to these five sections. Do not add figure or table lists, glossaries, page menus, or the original paper's section menu. Each section may contain multiple source cards. Place source sections and page numbers in card metadata, without turning them into additional sections or menu entries. The fixed headings are the user's organizational scheme. Do not present them as the paper's original sections.

Every summary item must be traceable to a statement in the original paper, with length adjusted to the paper's complexity. Preserve numerical values, units, conditions, and source locations for key quantitative conclusions, without assembling numbers detached from their conditions. When the authors report only a correlation, propose a mechanistic hypothesis, or use uncertain wording, preserve their expression without strengthening, correcting, or evaluating their conclusions.

## Stage 2 — Figure-by-figure and panel-by-panel restatement

**Inspect the original figures first, then cross-check them against captions, the main text, and relevant methods.** Use the images to accurately restate labeled information. Do not independently identify trends or derive conclusions. If tools cannot display an image or its resolution is insufficient, identify the affected figure or panel in the chat. Readable captions and explicit statements in the main text may still be restated, without claiming to have clearly inspected the original figure.

Organize `figures.html` by individual figure. Top-level headings and left-hand navigation correspond one-to-one to the original figure numbers, such as `Fig. 1` and `Fig. 2`, in source order, without renumbering. Give each figure its own group, source cards, and stable anchor, such as `fig-1` or `fig-2`. Navigation lists individual figures. Do not replace figure groups with topics based on source sections, pages, methods, or conclusions. Keep all panel restatements within their parent figure, retaining original labels such as a and b. Do not list panels as navigation entries alongside whole figures.

Cover all obtained main-text figures and their panels, including graphical abstracts and standalone schematics. Retain original numbering such as `Fig. S1` for supplied supplementary figures, with each figure listed under its own number in the menu. Identify genuinely unnumbered graphics by their original names, without inventing Fig. 0 or disrupting the main-text figure numbering. The file contains only figure restatements. Do not add sections for research background, an overall paper summary, terminology, or table analysis. Include complete tables in the full parallel text.

For each figure, include only the following information explicitly provided by labels, captions, or the relevant main text. Omit topics the paper does not address.

1. **Purpose stated by the authors.** Restate the authors' description of the purpose of the figure or associated experiment.
2. **Figure elements.** Describe groups, controls, treatments, colors, symbols, axes, units, scales, and legends. For microscopy images, include channels, markers, scale bars, and quantification methods. For schematics, include nodes, arrows, and meanings supplied by the authors. Describe only labeled or defined content, without adding a general figure-reading tutorial.
3. **Panel-by-panel restatement.** Use original labels such as Fig. 2a and 2b to separately restate the experiments, results, and explanations reported by the authors. Do not replace all panels with a single overall statement. If the authors provide no textual explanation for a panel, restate only its readable labels and relevant caption, without independently extracting findings from plotted trends or image appearance.
4. **Original statistical information.** Preserve the authors' n, replicate types, error-bar definitions, statistical tests, comparison groups, numerical values, and meanings of significance symbols. Restate only defined information, without guessing symbols, recalculating, or evaluating statistical results.
5. **The authors' conclusions and explanations.** Restate conclusions, explanations, or limitations explicitly associated with the figure by the authors. Describe connections to other figures only when the authors explicitly state them, without adding mechanisms, logical links, or inferences such as “this demonstrates.”

Where possible, embed clear original figures or complete crops in the figure HTML, retaining labels and legends and noting source page numbers. Embed images for offline use through data URIs or equivalent methods, without delivering separate image files. Do not redraw, add to, or beautify the original data. When file size or tool limitations prevent embedding images, use precise page and figure references and explain in the chat that the original figures are not embedded.

## Stage 3 — Full English–Chinese parallel text

Produce a complete translation suitable for paragraph-by-paragraph close reading. A Chinese summary, paraphrased outline, or figure explanation must not be presented as a full translation.

The body structure and left-hand navigation in `bilingual.html` follow the original paper's table of contents, preserving section and subsection numbering, names, hierarchy, and order. Menu labels may include Chinese translations of section names. Do not replace them with the summary's five headings or a figure list. If the paper has no separate contents page, build the menu from the actual section and subsection headings in its main text. Use PDF page navigation only when the original has no reliable heading structure. Retain body anchors and location identifiers for figures, tables, paragraphs, and pages as needed, without adding parallel navigation groups such as “all figures and tables” or “key terminology.”

### Alignment and completeness

- By default, include the title, abstract, all main-text sections, methods including methods placed at the end, conclusions, figure titles and captions, tables and table notes, footnotes, acknowledgments, declarations, and supplied appendices or supplementary materials. Preserve the original bibliographic details and numbering of references, without translating each entry by default. Report any excluded parts in the chat.
- Preserve the original section order and paragraph hierarchy, pairing each English source passage with its Chinese translation. Use paragraphs as the default unit, without merging multiple original paragraphs into a single summarized translation. Split long paragraphs only when the correspondence remains clear.
- Assign stable paragraph identifiers and record the source section or PDF page number. Page ranges may be used for paragraphs spanning pages. Keep figure, table, and equation numbers and in-text citations consistent to support cross-referencing between figure explanations, the translation, and the original paper.
- Repair obvious line-break hyphenation, repeated headers and footers, and extraction-order errors. Do not use these repairs to rewrite the English argument. Preserve locations for passages that cannot be reliably recognized and mark them “原文识别不清” (“source text unclear”), without filling them in independently.
- Preserve table row and column correspondence, numerical values, units, and notes. English text and its Chinese translation may share a cell. Provide complete parallel captions, including their full text. When necessary, list translations of English labels within figures separately, preserving the original images.

### Translation fidelity

- Accurately preserve negation, comparison direction, conditions, research facts conveyed by tense, and degrees of uncertainty expressed by words such as may and suggest. Do not strengthen “suggests” into “proves.”
- Maintain consistent technical terminology. At first mention, use “Chinese (English, abbreviation)” for key terms, then use the same Chinese rendering consistently. Retain abbreviations only when given in the original paper. If compiling a glossary, include only terms appearing in the source and their Chinese equivalents, without adding definitions or background absent from the paper.
- Preserve gene and protein nomenclature, compound names, model names, abbreviations, numbers, units, subscripts and superscripts, Greek letters, equations, and citation relationships. Preserve symbols within equations. Explanations following equations may be translated.
- Do not add reading tips, background, questions, commentary, or deductions from the translator, including material separately labeled as translator's notes. Translate the original authors' own notes normally and preserve their attribution.

### Parallel layout

Use the same HTML styling rules for the full English–Chinese parallel text, the summary, and the figure restatements. Place each paragraph pair in its own white `.reading-card`. At the top, use `.card-meta` for the paragraph identifier and source page numbers, followed by a `.parallel-row` containing `.parallel-cell[lang="en"]` and `.parallel-cell[lang="zh-CN"]`. On wide screens, show English on the left and Chinese on the right. On narrow screens, show English followed by Chinese for each paragraph. Do not place the entire English text and the entire Chinese translation in separate long columns, which would lose paragraph alignment. Group the cards by original section or page, with a sidebar menu, paragraph anchors, and figure identifiers for locating content.

Use an offline-capable method to display equations. When complex equations cannot be typeset reliably, embed numbered original images or retain readable equation text without hiding recognition errors behind the rendered layout.

## Consistent HTML styling for all deliverables

- Deliver the summary, figure restatements, full English–Chinese parallel text, and any additional reading materials as UTF-8 `.html` files. Do not automatically fall back to or additionally deliver Markdown, Word, PDF, plain text, standalone CSS, images, or scripts. Keep temporary extraction and checking files in the working directory. The original paper is an input and is outside this delivery-format restriction. Change the format only when the user subsequently requests it explicitly.
- Each HTML file must be a complete document that opens independently offline, with `<!doctype html>`, `<html lang="zh-CN">`, a UTF-8 charset declaration, and a viewport setting. Embed CSS, images, and necessary equation resources, with no dependency on CDNs, web fonts, or another file. If the environment imposes actual limitations, explain the specific limitations without silently changing formats.
- Before generating any file, read [assets/reading.css](assets/reading.css) and embed it unchanged in `<style data-literature-reading>`. Use exactly the same CSS for all three stages, without designing separate styles or overriding the layout through additional stylesheets, inline `style` attributes, or scripts. If the user requests styling changes during the same reading task, create a shared copy of this CSS in the task's working directory, edit that copy, and embed the updated copy in every file, including stage files already delivered. Do not modify the installed skill or deliver standalone CSS as part of this change.
- Before generating each HTML file, read [references/layout.md](references/layout.md). Follow its DOM structure for `.reading-shell`, the sidebar, and `<main class="reading-document">`. All three files use a light-gray background, white rounded content cards, pale-cyan source metadata bars, and teal separators to divide the text into readable groups. Use single-column card bodies for the summary and figure restatements and paragraph-by-paragraph two-column card bodies for the full parallel text. Keep text styling consistent for headings at the same level, body text, captions, and tables.
- Keep “阅读导航” fixed to the left side of the screen in every file, including on narrow screens. Do not move it to the top, right, or above the body. Long menus scroll independently inside the sidebar. Reserve the sidebar width and column gap to its right so the body remains clear of navigation during scrolling and anchor jumps. On narrow screens, reduce only the sidebar width and gap. The menu may collapse inside the left sidebar while retaining its position. Support native anchor links, keyboard operation, and “返回目录.” Basic navigation must work offline and without scripts.
- Use each file's prescribed organization. The summary uses the five headings above, figure restatements use the original figure numbers, and the full parallel text uses the original table of contents. Keep these menu structures specific to their respective files. Retain source sections, page numbers, card separation, and whitespace within groups. Keep paragraphs spanning pages and English–Chinese paragraph pairs intact across page groups, labeling their source with page ranges. Long sections in the parallel text may contain internal page groups, without adding top-level navigation entries when an original table of contents exists.
- Preserve source locations for cards within the five summary sections, cards within each figure, and original-section or page cards in the full parallel text. Do not invent a one-to-one source attribution for an item spanning multiple sections. Menu links must target content present in the same file, including the five required summary headings. Do not copy paper titles, headings, or terms from reference screenshots, or link to files that have yet to be generated.

## Long papers and delivery checks

- Write long papers to files progressively by section, maintaining section, figure, table, and paragraph coverage records and a glossary to prevent omissions or duplicate translations across context changes. Do not substitute a summary because of a single-message length limit or label a partial translation as complete.
- If actual execution limits prevent completion in one session, deliver the completed files and identify covered sections, remaining work, and the continuation point. Do not imply that the remaining work is already finished.
- Before delivery, check the coverage inventory. All figures and panels must be covered according to the source, without filling gaps with independent explanations. Every original paragraph must have a corresponding translation. Methods, captions, tables, and supplementary materials must have no silent omissions. Pay particular attention to values, units, negation, equation numbers, and citations.
- Check that every substantive statement in the summary and figure explanations has an explicit source in the paper. Remove outside knowledge, independent calculations, inferences, evaluations, reading advice, and lists of matters the paper does not address. Keep the translator's views out of the translation.
- Check that top-level body groups correspond exactly to the sidebar menu. The summary has exactly “研究背景、研究内容、研究方法、研究目的、研究结论” in that order. Figure restatements correspond to the original figure numbers, with panels kept inside their parent figures. The parallel text menu preserves original section numbering, hierarchy, and order, without incorporating headings from the other files. Leave sections without source support empty.
- Spot-check that paired English text remains faithful to the original and that the Chinese is accurate. Verify important quantitative conclusions against the original pages. For HTML, inspect long paragraphs, wide tables, equations, embedded images, offline opening, and narrow-screen alignment. Compare the main titles, section headings, body paragraphs, captions, and tables across all three files, checking that they use the same CSS and have no local layout overrides. When browser preview is available, verify the actual computed fonts, font sizes, indentation, alignment, and line heights to catch interference from browser defaults. Also check that every menu anchor exists and is unique, follows the file's prescribed order, and that section or page groups keep paragraph pairs intact. At every screen width, navigation must stay fixed on the left and clear of the body on the right. On narrow screens, verify that the menu collapses inside the left sidebar and the overall page has no horizontal overflow. Wide tables must scroll horizontally only within their containers. Clearly state any checks that could not be performed.
- Save the three HTML files, `summary.html`, `figures.html`, and `bilingual.html`, in the user-specified directory or the current environment's delivery directory. Use separate subdirectories for multiple papers, and deliver any additional reading materials as HTML as well. At final delivery, link only to these HTML reading files and explain coverage or missing materials, without linking internal intermediate files or pasting the entire translation again.
