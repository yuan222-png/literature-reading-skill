# literature reading

[简体中文](SKILL.md) · English

Support this reading pattern. The user first reads an AI summary, then browses the paper's figures independently. Codex continues with figure-reading guides, faithful restatements of the paper's analysis, and the full English–Chinese parallel text for the user to return to and read closely. Use Simplified Chinese for summaries, guides, and restatements, retaining essential English terminology.

## Content boundaries

- Chinese summaries, full parallel translations, and study-specific results, analysis, and conclusions in the figure file use only the current paper, its labels and captions, and obtained appendices or supplementary materials. Present only explicit source information and the authors' expressed explanations, views, and conclusions.
- Only the “图表导读” (Figure-reading guide) portion of `figures.html` may add researched foundational knowledge needed to understand a figure, including terminology, symbols, metrics, visual encodings, and basic principles. Cite sources and separate this knowledge from “文献中的分析与结论” (Analysis and conclusions in the paper). Do not add outside knowledge to the summary or parallel translation, independently review the study, or expand the guide into an unrelated survey.
- Do not derive study results, complete causal chains, explain study-specific mechanisms, establish relationships between figures, or propose applications independently. A plausible or calculable judgment is not the authors' conclusion. Preserve original values without computing differences, ratios, effect sizes, or estimating values from plots.
- Do not independently evaluate novelty, evidence strength, quality, design, limitations, applicability, or what the figure cannot establish. Restate these only when the authors explicitly discuss them, with attribution.
- Omit study-specific facts, results, or conclusions absent from the paper; do not add missing-information checklists or fill sections by speculation. This does not prohibit sourced general knowledge in figure guides. Retain all five fixed summary headings and anchors, leaving unsupported bodies empty. Preserve original ambiguity.
- Preserve attribution, hypotheses, speculation, uncertainty, and inferences made by the authors themselves. Do not strengthen speculation into established fact.
- Include only content permitted for each stage and necessary headings, identifiers, page references, and navigation. External foundational knowledge stays within the relevant figure guide. Briefly report execution limitations in chat rather than turning them into commentary on the paper.

## Execution pace and scope

- By default, complete the sequence continuously, from summary to figure-reading guides with faithful restatement of the paper's analysis, then to the full English–Chinese parallel text. Immediately save the completed summary as `summary.html` and provide its link in the chat, then continue with the remaining two stages. Provide `figures.html` when the figure guides and restatements are complete and `bilingual.html` when the full parallel text is complete. A brief summary may also appear in the chat. Do not wait until the entire translation is finished to deliver the summary, or require the user to reply “continue” or confirm that they have finished viewing the figures between stages.
- Follow explicit requests for a particular stage, selected sections, a different order, or a specified format, without forcing the full workflow. When reusing existing summaries or figure explanations, verify the paper version and remove outside content that violates the stage-specific boundaries and all independent judgments; retain legitimate sourced figure-guide knowledge.
- Continue making progress within the current task. Do not claim that a background task has started or that work will continue after the user leaves unless such an execution mechanism actually exists.
- Prefer the PDF, attachments, or full-text link supplied by the user. If only an abstract is available, a summary explicitly limited to the abstract may be provided first. Clearly identify unavailable full text or images and request the corresponding materials, without fabricating subsequent figure explanations or a full translation.
- When handling multiple papers, organize each paper's sources, summary, figure explanations, and translation separately. Clarify priorities when the number of papers affects delivery order.

## Reading and locating the source

1. Confirm the title, authors, year, any existing DOI or link, and the version used from the source materials. The full text of the same paper may be obtained. Do not expand background for summaries or translations; follow Stage 2 for figure-guide research. Read the full-text structure, key methods, results, discussion, and captions before forming a summary. Do not present findings based only on an abstract as conclusions from reading the full paper.
2. Maintain a concise coverage inventory of sections, the location of methods, figure and panel numbers, tables, appendices, and supplied supplementary materials. Use it to prevent omissions without overwhelming the user with the extraction process.
3. Combine PDF text extraction with page images. Check two-column reading order, paragraphs spanning pages, ligatures, footnotes, and equations. Scanned documents may require OCR first. Check uncertain numbers, symbols, and figure labels against the page images.
4. Provide retrievable source locations for important conclusions and every figure, such as “PDF page 7, Fig. 3b.” When printed page numbers differ from PDF page order, specify which numbering is used.
5. Include supplementary materials in restatements and translations only when they have actually been obtained and belong to the current paper. Mention unavailable materials in the chat. Preserve the original attribution of cited papers. Do not use outside results to reevaluate or rewrite this study's evidence. Figure guides may consult relevant original methods or foundational concepts as separately attributed knowledge explanations.

## Stage 1 — Chinese summary

The top-level content sections and left-hand navigation in `summary.html` must use exactly the following five Chinese labels in the same order. Keep their names and order fixed, with no additional content sections.

1. **研究背景 (Research background)**. Restate the background, prior work, questions, and gaps described by the authors.
2. **研究内容 (Research content)**. Restate the research undertaken, subjects, tasks, and work performed by the authors, explaining what they studied.
3. **研究方法 (Research methods)**. Restate the data, samples, experiments, models, technical procedures, controls, and evaluation methods used by the authors, explaining how they conducted the study.
4. **研究目的 (Research objectives)**. Restate research objectives or problems explicitly identified by the authors, without inferring objectives from methods or results.
5. **研究结论 (Research conclusions)**. Restate the main findings and explicit conclusions reported by the authors. Retain explicitly stated significance, qualifying conditions, or limitations alongside the relevant conclusions, without creating separate sections for novelty, significance, or limitations.

Use `summary-background`, `summary-content`, `summary-methods`, `summary-objectives`, and `summary-conclusions` as the respective section anchors. Navigation links only to these five sections. Do not add figure or table lists, glossaries, page menus, or the original paper's section menu. Each section may contain multiple source cards. Place source sections and page numbers in card metadata, without turning them into additional sections or menu entries. The fixed headings are the user's organizational scheme. Do not present them as the paper's original sections.

Every summary item must be traceable to a statement in the original paper, with length adjusted to the paper's complexity. Preserve numerical values, units, conditions, and source locations for key quantitative conclusions, without assembling numbers detached from their conditions. When the authors report only a correlation, propose a mechanistic hypothesis, or use uncertain wording, preserve their expression without strengthening, correcting, or evaluating their conclusions.

## Stage 2 — Figure-reading guides and faithful restatement of the paper's analysis

Enable readers to understand the figure's objects, encodings, symbols, and necessary concepts from the figure and this file without repeatedly consulting the paper. Interpretation of research findings must follow the paper entirely. **Inspect the original figure and cross-check its captions, main text, and methods.** Report unreadable images or insufficient resolution in chat; do not claim to see details or fill them using external knowledge.

Keep the existing structure of `figures.html`: one top-level heading and sidebar entry per original figure (`Fig. 1`, `Fig. 2`, etc.), in original order, with stable anchors such as `fig-1`. Keep panels a, b, and so on inside their parent figure without separate sidebar entries. Include obtained graphical abstracts, standalone schematics, and supplementary figures under their original labels; do not invent numbering for unnumbered graphics. Do not add separate top-level summaries, terminology topics, or table-analysis sections. Complete tables remain in the full parallel text.

### Separate reading guidance from the authors' analysis within every figure

Within each figure group, place the original figure inside a white-backed `.figure-plate` (which supports pure-CSS `:target` full-screen zoom), then clearly separate `.figure-block[data-kind="guide"]` (“图表导读”, Figure-reading guide) and `.figure-block[data-kind="analysis"]` (“文献中的分析与结论”, Analysis and conclusions in the paper, styled with a soft pine-green plate). Place external foundational knowledge inside `.knowledge-note` callouts with an ochre left rule, and mark panel headings with `.panel-label` badges. Do not add sidebar entries for these internal parts. Adapt content to the actual figure type; do not force nonexistent curves or statistical tests into the explanation.

**Figure-reading guide.** Use the image, caption, main text, methods, and targeted research as needed to explain, including but not limited to:

- **Objects and experimental context:** identify the subjects, task, conditions, groups, and controls using the paper's own information. Restate needed context locally rather than only directing the reader back to the paper.
- **Axes, curves, and points:** explain axis variables, units, ranges, and linear/logarithmic scales. Map each curve's color and line style to its object. Explain point shapes, colors, sizes, what one point represents (sample, measurement, mean, or another object), and how connecting or fitted lines are defined. Study-specific mappings must come from this paper's legend, caption, or methods, not conventions guessed from other sources.
- **Symbols and annotations:** explain arrows, shading, error bars, confidence intervals, reference lines, abbreviations, and significance symbols. Distinguish this paper's definitions from general meanings discussed elsewhere. Explain microscopy channels, markers, and scale bars, or schematic nodes, modules, and links, as relevant.
- **Necessary foundations:** explain metric definitions, formula symbols, basic measurement or plotting principles, and how to read the encodings. Sourced knowledge may explain how to read the plot; it must not decide which method wins in this figure, why its trend occurs, or what the study's results imply.
- **Panel correspondence:** retain original a/b labels and explain each panel's encoding and legend. Make necessary terminology available within the figure guide instead of requiring another reading file.

**Analysis and conclusions in the paper.** Restate only the authors' explicit purpose, results, explanations, statistics, conclusions, and qualifications for this figure or panel, citing the main-text/caption location. Preserve uncertainty and attribution such as “the authors report/explain.” Do not add comparisons, trend judgments, mechanisms, causal interpretations, quality rankings, or conclusions not made by the authors. Do not infer new findings from curve shapes, point distributions, or outside knowledge. If there is no textual analysis, retain existing caption information and explicit labels without inventing analysis to fill this part.

### Research, sources, and uncertainty

- Confirm study-specific information in the paper first. Research only general concepts necessary for reading the figure, preferring original method papers, textbooks, standards, official documentation, or authoritative institutions. Actually open and verify sources; do not describe snippets or model memory as checked evidence. Do not search later work to revise or evaluate this paper's findings.
- Clearly label “本篇定义/图示信息” (Definitions or information in this paper) and “基础知识补充（外部来源）” (Foundational knowledge from external sources). Attach traceable citations or source titles and links next to external explanations, without adding a top-level references or foundation menu. Embed explanation text for offline reading; source links are optional verification aids.
- A general definition cannot substitute for this paper's custom symbols, algorithms, metrics, or settings. Never assign a common meaning to an unconfirmed study-specific label. A generic definition may be introduced as generic without claiming the paper uses it. Briefly disclose unresolved items affecting figure comprehension in chat.
- If research is unavailable or reliable sources cannot be found, state the limitation without fabricating sources, and complete the guide from available paper information. Preserve original values and symbols without computing differences, effects, fitting parameters, or fabricated numerical examples to derive study results.

Where possible, embed clear original figures or complete crops for offline reading, retaining labels, legends, and source page numbers. Do not redraw or modify original data. If embedding is limited, provide precise figure/page locations and report the limitation in chat.

## Stage 3 — Full English–Chinese parallel text

Produce a complete translation suitable for paragraph-by-paragraph close reading. A Chinese summary, paraphrased outline, or figure explanation must not be presented as a full translation.

The body structure and left-hand navigation in `bilingual.html` follow the original paper's table of contents, preserving section and subsection numbering, names, hierarchy, and order. Menu labels may include Chinese translations of section names. Do not replace them with the summary's five headings or a figure list. If the paper has no separate contents page, build the menu from the actual section and subsection headings in its main text. Use PDF page navigation only when the original has no reliable heading structure. Retain body anchors and location identifiers for figures, tables, paragraphs, and pages as needed, without adding parallel navigation groups such as “all figures and tables” or “key terminology.”

### Alignment and completeness

- By default, include the title, abstract, all main-text sections, methods including methods placed at the end, conclusions, figure titles and captions, tables and table notes, footnotes, acknowledgments, declarations, and supplied appendices or supplementary materials. Preserve the original bibliographic details and numbering of references, without translating each entry by default. Report any excluded parts in the chat.
- Preserve the original section order and paragraph hierarchy, pairing each English source passage with its Chinese translation. Use paragraphs as the default unit, without merging multiple original paragraphs into a single summarized translation. Split long paragraphs only when the correspondence remains clear.
- Assign stable paragraph identifiers and record the source section or PDF page number. Page ranges may be used for paragraphs spanning pages. Keep figure, table, and equation numbers and in-text citations consistent to support cross-referencing between figure explanations, the translation, and the original paper.
- Repair obvious line-break hyphenation, repeated headers and footers, and extraction-order errors. Do not use these repairs to rewrite the English argument. Preserve locations for passages that cannot be reliably recognized and mark them “原文识别不清” (“source text unclear”), without filling them in independently.
- Preserve table row and column correspondence, numerical values, units, and notes. English text and its Chinese translation may share a cell. Provide complete parallel captions, including their full text. When necessary, list translations of English labels within figures separately, preserving the original images.

### Translation fidelity

- Accurately preserve negation, comparison direction, conditions, research facts conveyed by tense, and degrees of uncertainty expressed by words such as may and suggest. Do not strengthen “suggests” into “proves.”
- Maintain consistent technical terminology. At first mention, use “Chinese (English, abbreviation)” for key terms, then use the same Chinese rendering consistently. Retain abbreviations only when given in the original paper. If compiling a glossary, include only terms appearing in the source and their Chinese equivalents, without adding definitions or background absent from the paper.
- Preserve gene and protein nomenclature, compound names, model names, abbreviations, numbers, units, subscripts and superscripts, Greek letters, equations, and citation relationships. Preserve symbols within equations. Explanations following equations may be translated.
- Do not add reading tips, background, questions, commentary, or deductions from the translator, including material separately labeled as translator's notes. Translate the original authors' own notes normally and preserve their attribution.

### Parallel layout

Use the same HTML styling rules for the full English–Chinese parallel text, the summary, and the figure restatements, setting `data-document="bilingual"` on `<body>`. Place each paragraph pair in its own warm-white `.reading-card`. At the top, use `.card-meta` for the paragraph identifier and source page numbers, followed by a `.parallel-row` containing `.parallel-cell[lang="en"]` and `.parallel-cell[lang="zh-CN"]`. Adjacent paragraph cards within a section join into a continuous parallel ledger: on wide screens, `.card-meta` moves into a left margin column while the right side shows English on the left (academic serif, left-aligned, unindented) and Chinese on the right (sans-serif, 2em first-line indent), displaying the language column labels only on the first card of each group to avoid visual noise; on narrow screens, each paragraph stacks English followed by Chinese. Do not place the entire English text and the entire Chinese translation in separate long columns, which would lose paragraph alignment. Group the cards by original section or page, with a sidebar menu, paragraph anchors, and figure identifiers for locating content.

Use an offline-capable method to display equations. When complex equations cannot be typeset reliably, embed numbered original images or retain readable equation text without hiding recognition errors behind the rendered layout.

## Consistent HTML styling for all deliverables

The original paper is an input and is outside this delivery-format restriction. Change the format only when the user subsequently requests it explicitly.

Embed CSS, the progressive-enhancement script, images, and necessary equation resources, so displaying the content has no dependency on CDNs, web fonts, or another file. If the environment imposes actual limitations, explain the specific limitations without silently changing formats.

Use exactly the same CSS and script for all three stages, without designing separate styles or overriding the layout through additional stylesheets, inline `style` attributes, or scripts. If the user requests styling changes during the same reading task, create a shared copy of this CSS in the task's working directory, edit that copy, and embed the updated copy in every file, including stage files already delivered. ## Long papers and delivery checks

- Write long papers to files progressively by section, maintaining section, figure, table, and paragraph coverage records and a glossary to prevent omissions or duplicate translations across context changes. Do not substitute a summary because of a single-message length limit or label a partial translation as complete.
- If actual execution limits prevent completion in one session, deliver the completed files and identify covered sections, remaining work, and the continuation point. Do not imply that the remaining work is already finished.
- Before delivery, check the coverage inventory. Cover figure information, reading guidance, and the authors' existing analysis for all figures and panels without independently deriving missing study results. Every original paragraph must have a corresponding translation. Methods, captions, tables, and supplementary materials must have no silent omissions. Pay particular attention to values, units, negation, equation numbers, and citations.
- Check stage-specific source boundaries: summaries, translations, study facts, and authors' analyses require explicit paper sources; external foundational knowledge in guides requires verified, clearly marked sources. Retain legitimate sourced guidance, but never assign outside definitions to this paper without confirmation. Remove independent calculations, deductions, evaluations, missing-study-information lists, and translator opinions. For every figure, ensure specific curves, points, and symbols map to this paper's definitions and that guidance has not turned into claims about what the figure proves.
- Check that top-level body groups correspond exactly to the sidebar menu. The summary has exactly “研究背景、研究内容、研究方法、研究目的、研究结论” in that order. Figure restatements correspond to the original figure numbers, with panels kept inside their parent figures. The parallel text menu preserves original section numbering, hierarchy, and order, without incorporating headings from the other files. Leave sections without source support empty.
- Check the navigation in each title card. The current-page indicator must be correct and links must point to generated files for the same paper in the same directory. For complete delivery, click both outgoing links from each HTML file and confirm all six directions open the target main page in the current tab, including offline.
- Spot-check that paired English text remains faithful to the original and that the Chinese is accurate. Verify important quantitative conclusions against the original pages. For HTML, inspect long paragraphs, wide tables, equations, embedded images, offline opening, and narrow-screen alignment. Compare the main titles, section headings, body paragraphs, captions, and tables across all three files, checking that they use the same CSS and have no local layout overrides. When browser preview is available, verify the actual computed fonts, font sizes, indentation, alignment, and line heights to catch interference from browser defaults. Also check that every menu anchor exists and is unique, follows the file's prescribed order, and that section or page groups keep paragraph pairs intact. At every screen width, navigation must stay fixed on the left and clear of the body on the right. On narrow screens, verify that the menu collapses inside the left sidebar and the overall page has no horizontal overflow. Wide tables must scroll horizontally only within their containers. Clearly state any checks that could not be performed.
- Save the three HTML files, `summary.html`, `figures.html`, and `bilingual.html`, in the user-specified directory or the current environment's delivery directory. Use separate subdirectories for multiple papers, and deliver any additional reading materials as HTML as well. At final delivery, link only to these HTML reading files and explain coverage or missing materials, without linking internal intermediate files or pasting the entire translation again.
